Glomerular Disease & Nephrotic Syndrome

Protein & Blood in Urine

Urine should normally be free from significant amounts of protein and red blood cells. The presence of foamy urine (proteinuria) or red/tea-colored urine (hematuria) is often the earliest red flag signaling inflammation or breach in the kidney’s microscopic filters (glomeruli).

Understanding Glomerular Filtration Disorders

How immune, genetic, and systemic conditions compromise the renal filtration barrier.

🌊

Nephrotic Syndrome (Heavy Protein Leakage)

When the specialized filtration cells (podocytes) are injured, large quantities of albumin escape into the urine (> 3.5 grams per 24 hours):

  • Loss of blood albumin lowers oncotic pressure, causing intense fluid retention and swelling (facial puffiness, swollen feet and abdomen).
  • Compensatory liver production of lipids leads to severely elevated blood cholesterol.
  • Common etiologies include Minimal Change Disease (MCD), Focal Segmental Glomerulosclerosis (FSGS), and Membranous Nephropathy.
🩸

Nephritic Syndrome & Glomerulonephritis

Characterized by active inflammation within the glomerular capillaries:

  • Inflammatory cells puncture capillary walls, allowing red blood cells to enter urine (dysmorphic RBCs and red cell casts).
  • Urine may appear dark brown, cola-colored, or frankly red.
  • Often accompanied by sudden high blood pressure, fluid retention, and declining kidney filtration.
  • Common etiologies include IgA Nephropathy, Lupus Nephritis, ANCA vasculitis, and post-infectious glomerulonephritis.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Clinical indicators that you should undergo immediate nephrological testing:

  • Bubbly, Frothy Urine: Foam in the toilet that resembles whipped egg whites and requires multiple flushes to clear.
  • Periorbital Edema: Marked puffiness around the eyes, particularly prominent when waking up in the morning.
  • Rapid Swelling & Weight Gain: Lower leg swelling (pitting edema) or sudden abdominal distension (ascites) over days.
  • Abnormal Urine Color: Urine that turns pink, smokey brown, or dark tea-colored (gross hematuria).
  • Sudden Elevation in Blood Pressure: Spikes in BP occurring alongside urine changes.

How Dr. Anil Kumar Helps

A targeted, diagnostic pathway to identify the root cause and arrest damage:

  • Quantitative Laboratory Profiling: 24-hour urine protein or spot Urine Protein-to-Creatinine Ratio (uPCR), accompanied by phase-contrast microscopic urinalysis.
  • Immunological & Autoimmune Panel: Screening for ANA, anti-dsDNA, complement levels (C3/C4), anti-PLA2R antibodies, and ANCA vasculitis markers.
  • Ultrasound-Guided Kidney Biopsy: Coordinating precision percutaneous renal biopsies under ultrasound guidance, allowing specialized histopathology, immunofluorescence, and electron microscopy.
  • Targeted Immunomodulation: Individualized therapies including corticosteroids, calcineurin inhibitors (Tacrolimus/Cyclosporine), or biological agents (Rituximab) to halt glomerular inflammation.
  • Swelling & Lipid Control: Judicious diuretic regimes, ACEi/ARB therapy to reduce proteinuria, and statin management for secondary hyperlipidemia.
Seek Immediate Urgent Care If: You notice sudden, rapid whole-body swelling accompanied by breathing difficulty or chest congestion (indicating fluid in the lungs), dark tea-colored urine accompanied by high fever, or severe sudden headaches with sky-high blood pressure. Proceed without delay to the nearest hospital emergency room or call 108.
