Renal Replacement Therapy

Dialysis Planning & Care

When advanced kidney disease significantly impairs the body's natural filtration capacity, dialysis performs the essential work of removing toxins, excess water, and balancing electrolytes. Planned, timely preparation ensures comfort, safety, and a much higher quality of life.

Understanding Dialysis Modalities

Choosing the right therapy according to medical condition, cardiac health, and lifestyle.

🏥

Hemodialysis (HD)

Blood is gently drawn from a vascular access, circulated through a dialyzer (artificial kidney) where toxins and excess fluid are filtered out across a semipermeable membrane, and returned safely to your body.

  • Usually performed 2 to 3 sessions per week at a dialysis center.
  • Each session lasts approximately 4 hours under trained nurse & technician care.
  • Requires permanent vascular access, ideally an Arteriovenous (AV) Fistula created weeks ahead.
🏡

Peritoneal Dialysis (PD / CAPD)

Uses the natural lining of your abdomen (peritoneum) as a filter. A sterile dialysate fluid is introduced through a soft, flexible catheter, absorbs toxins over several hours, and is then drained.

  • Performed at home or workplace by the patient or caregiver.
  • No needles or vascular access punctures required.
  • Offers excellent mobility and travel flexibility, especially beneficial for elderly patients or those with frail veins.

Signs that Dialysis May Be Needed

When eGFR falls below 10–15 ml/min and medications can no longer maintain equilibrium:

  • Intractable Nausea & Vomiting: Inability to retain food or oral medications due to uremic toxin buildup.
  • Severe Fluid Congestion: Relentless breathlessness when lying down, pulmonary congestion, or massive leg edema unresponsive to diuretics.
  • Dangerous Electrolyte Spikes: Recurrent, critical hyperkalemia (high potassium) that poses serious cardiac arrhythmia risks.
  • Metabolic Acidosis: Severe acid buildup in the blood causing rapid, deep breathing and extreme exhaustion.
  • Uremic Encephalopathy: Confusion, altered sleep cycles, involuntary tremors (asterixis), or cognitive slowing.

How Dr. Anil Kumar Helps

Guiding patients and their families safely through every stage of dialysis:

  • Pre-emptive Vascular Planning: Timely referral for AV fistula creation well before symptoms start, eliminating the pain and risk of emergency central venous catheters.
  • Personalized Dialysis Prescription: Determining dialyzer membrane, blood flow rates, ultrafiltration targets, and anticoagulation protocols.
  • Dry Weight & BP Optimization: Fine-tuning your ideal target dry weight to prevent muscle cramps, drops in BP, or chronic heart strain.
  • Managing Complications: Specialized protocols for renal anemia, intravenous iron, calcium-phosphate binders, and infection prevention.
  • Center Coordination: Seamless clinical oversight and coordination with leading dialysis units across Kochi.
Dialysis Emergency Warnings: If you miss a scheduled dialysis session and develop shortness of breath, sudden chest pain, severe palpitations, sudden weakness, or if your AV fistula stops vibrating (loss of "thrill") or bleeds uncontrollably, seek emergency hospital care immediately. Fluid overload and cardiac arrest from high potassium can develop rapidly. Call 108 or go to your dialysis emergency room.
