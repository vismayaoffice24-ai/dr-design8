Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

Chronic Kidney Disease refers to the gradual, progressive loss of renal filtration capacity over months or years. Because the early stages typically produce zero noticeable symptoms, proactive staging and specialized medical care are essential to protect remaining nephrons and avoid renal failure.

Understanding Chronic Kidney Disease

How kidneys lose filtration capacity and why early detection changes everything.

What Happens in CKD?

Healthy kidneys contain millions of microscopic filtering units called nephrons that filter waste products and excess fluids from the blood into urine. In CKD, ongoing conditions such as diabetes, high blood pressure, glomerulonephritis, or recurrent infections steadily damage these filters.

As functioning nephrons diminish, the remaining ones are forced to work overtime under higher pressure, accelerating wear and tear. Without expert intervention, creatinine accumulates in the bloodstream, electrolyte and fluid balances are disrupted, and systemic complications arise.

The 5 Stages of CKD (Based on eGFR)

eGFR indicates milliliters of blood filtered per minute:

Stage 1: Kidney damage with normal function eGFR 90+
Stage 2: Mild loss of kidney function eGFR 60–89
Stage 3a & 3b: Moderate loss of function eGFR 30–59
Stage 4: Severe reduction in filtration eGFR 15–29
Stage 5: Kidney failure (ESRD) eGFR < 15

Warning Signs to Watch For

Symptoms typically appear as kidney damage advances towards Stage 3 and beyond:

  • Fluid Retention & Swelling: Noticeable swelling in the feet, ankles, shins, or puffy bags under the eyes in the morning.
  • Changes in Urination: Passing foamy, bubbly urine, urinating multiple times during the night (nocturia), or a decrease in urine output.
  • Profound Fatigue & Anemia: Feeling constantly exhausted due to reduced erythropoietin (a kidney hormone needed to make red blood cells).
  • Appetite & Digestive Changes: Loss of appetite, a metallic taste in the mouth, nausea, or aversion to protein-rich foods.
  • Severe Dry Skin & Itching: Unexplained persistent itching caused by high phosphorus and mineral imbalances.
  • Difficult-to-Control BP: Blood pressure that continues to climb despite multiple antihypertensive tablets.

How Dr. Anil Kumar Helps

A structured, evidence-based management plan to preserve your kidney function:

  • Root Cause Identification: Investigating whether diabetes, renovascular disease, medications, or glomerulonephritis is the primary driver.
  • Kidney-Protective Medication Protocols: Initiating renin-angiotensin inhibitors (ACEi/ARBs), SGLT2 inhibitors, and non-steroidal MRAs proven to slow eGFR decline.
  • Renal Anemia & Mineral Bone Care: Monitoring and correcting iron deficiency, prescribing erythropoietin-stimulating agents, and stabilizing phosphorus and calcium.
  • Custom Dietary & Fluid Prescription: Formulating individualized limits for dietary sodium, protein, potassium, and daily fluids.
  • Pre-emptive Renal Replacement Planning: If eGFR approaches advanced stages, guiding the patient early on vascular access (AV fistula) or transplant evaluation to prevent emergency dialysis.
Seek Immediate Urgent Care If: You or your family member experience sudden shortness of breath (especially when lying flat), severe chest tightness, sudden inability to produce urine, vomiting repeatedly, or experiencing severe confusion, drowsiness, or irregular heart rhythms. These are signs of acute fluid overload, severe acidosis, or life-threatening hyperkalemia requiring immediate casualty intervention. Call 108 or visit your nearest hospital.
