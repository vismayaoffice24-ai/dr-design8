Pre & Post-Transplant Nephrology

Kidney Transplant Care

Kidney transplantation represents the definitive gold standard for kidney failure, offering patients renewed energy, freedom from dialysis machines, and an active, vibrant life. Meticulous preparation and vigilant post-transplant monitoring ensure your new kidney lasts for decades.

The Kidney Transplant Pathway

Guiding patients through the two essential phases of renal transplantation.

📋

1. Pre-Transplant Evaluation

A comprehensive workup to ensure optimal safety for both recipient and donor:

  • Recipient Assessment: Rigorous evaluation of cardiovascular health, pulmonary reserve, occult infection screening, and immunological risk profile.
  • Living Donor Workup: Complete clinical, imaging, and psychological assessment to verify that donating a kidney will not impact the donor's long-term health.
  • Tissue Typing: ABO blood group matching, HLA tissue typing, panel reactive antibodies (PRA), and CDC/Flow crossmatches.
  • Regulatory Navigation: Clear guidance through statutory documentation under the Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act (THOTA).
🛡️

2. Post-Transplant Care

Long-term medical stewardship to guard against graft rejection and infections:

  • Immunosuppressive Titration: Precise therapeutic drug monitoring (measuring blood trough levels of Tacrolimus, Cyclosporine, or Mycophenolate).
  • Infection Prophylaxis: Strategic preventative medications against opportunistic infections like Cytomegalovirus (CMV), BK virus, and Pneumocystis (PCP).
  • Metabolic Management: Managing post-transplant diabetes mellitus (PTDM), hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and bone health.
  • Graft Surveillance: Immediate diagnostic evaluation and protocol kidney biopsies if creatinine fluctuates.

Warning Signs in Transplant Patients

Symptoms that demand immediate clinical review:

  • Graft Site Pain or Fullness: Tenderness, swelling, or aching discomfort directly over the transplanted kidney in the lower abdomen.
  • Drop in Urine Output: Passing visibly less urine over a 24-hour period despite drinking adequate fluids.
  • Unexplained Fever: Any temperature elevation above 100°F (37.8°C), night sweats, or chills.
  • Rising Serum Creatinine: Laboratory reports showing an unexpected climb in creatinine during regular follow-ups.
  • Sudden Fluid Retention: Puffy eyelids, ankle swelling, or gaining more than 1–2 kg over a few days.
  • Medication Side Effects: Hand tremors, gum swelling, stomach upset, or persistent diarrhea.

How Dr. Anil Kumar Helps

Expert guidance from preparation to decades of healthy graft life:

  • Fitness & Registry Guidance: Assessing clinical eligibility and facilitating registration on government deceased-donor organ registries (Mrithasanjeevani / KNOS).
  • Living Donor Counseling: Educating prospective family donors on surgical safety, recovery expectations, and lifelong renal health.
  • Precision Drug Titration: Balancing immunosuppression to prevent organ rejection while shielding the body against unwanted toxicities.
  • Rejection Management: Rapid diagnosis of cellular or antibody-mediated rejection (AMR) and coordinating prompt pulse steroid or intravenous immunoglobulin therapy.
  • Vaccination & Lifestyle Roadmap: Formulating safe, inactive vaccination schedules, exercise routines, and safe food-handling practices for immunosuppressed life.
Post-Transplant Emergency Warning: If you have received a kidney transplant and experience a fever, sudden pain or swelling over the graft site in the lower abdomen, a sharp decline in urine output, or a cough with breathlessness, treat this as an acute medical emergency. Do not adjust your anti-rejection medicines on your own. Contact Dr. Anil Kumar or proceed immediately to the transplant center casualty.
